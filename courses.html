Agentic AI courses elsewhere.

Universities started teaching agents as a subject of its own in 2024, and most of the good courses publish their syllabi, slides and sometimes lectures. This page collects the ones with a public website, says what each emphasizes, and notes how this course differs. Every link was opened and checked on the date above; course sites move, so tell me if one breaks.

How to read this list. Three families, in practice.
  • Systems and research courses (Stanford CS 329Z, CMU 11-768, Harvard CS 2680) assume a CS background and go down to training, serving and benchmarks.
  • Builder courses (Berkeley's MOOC, UVA, MIT's studio, Stanford CS 146S) are project-driven and lighter on theory.
  • Business-school courses (NYU Stern, and this one) start from the decision a manager has to make and work back to the mechanism.
If you want to go deeper after Week 14, the systems courses are where to go; if you want more practice, the builder courses.
University courses with public websites · 2025–2026

Ours is one path through the subject. Here are others.

Stanford · CS 329Z · Fall 2026

Engineering AI Agents

Diyi Yang, Michael Ryan, John Yang

The shift from single models to compound AI systems: students build RAG, tool use and the agent loop from scratch, then study how frameworks such as DSPy abstract them. Distinctive lectures: data for agentic systems (traces, flywheels, synthetic data), optimization (prompt optimization vs weights), LLM-as-judge and evaluation infrastructure, and proactive agents. Two applied homeworks and a quarter-long project; the first homework is a public evaluation harness.

Carnegie Mellon · 11-768 · Fall 2026

AI Agents

Daniel Fried, Graham Neubig (Language Technologies Institute)

LLM-based agents that perceive, reason, plan and act over many steps: calling tools, navigating interfaces, writing code, working alongside people. Covers the foundations of building agents and the open research questions around long-horizon reasoning, training, safety and interaction, with an emphasis on hands-on implementation and on the training methods that make agents more capable and reliable. Ends with a poster session in December. Neubig co-founded All Hands AI, the company behind the OpenHands coding agent, so that material is first-hand.

UC Berkeley RDI · MOOC · Fall 2025

Agentic AI

Dawn Song; Xinyun Chen (Meta, guest co-instructor); weekly guest lecturers

The third iteration of Berkeley RDI's open course (after LLM Agents, Fall 2024, and Advanced LLM Agents, Spring 2025). A lecture series by people who build the systems: OpenAI, Google DeepMind, NVIDIA, Microsoft, Meta, Sierra and Sony AI. Recordings are on YouTube and anyone may watch and audit; assignments are optional, and a companion AgentX competition runs alongside. The best single place to hear the industry's own framing of the field, and the least structured as a course.

Harvard · CS 2680 · Fall 2026

Modern AI Systems: Agents and System Optimizations

Juncheng Yang (MaDSys group)

Follows one agentic system from the agent loop down through the serving stack: students first use a modern coding agent (Claude Code), then design their own agent with provided API access, measure it, and optimize the systems underneath. Five assignments with a leaderboard and a final project. Prerequisites are real: CS61, one graduate systems course, and comfort with Python and PyTorch.

NYU Stern · Spring 2026 (second half)

Foundations of AI Agents

Designed by Srikanth Jagabathula, Ilan Lobel, Raveesh Mayya and Omar Mouchtaki (Technology, Operations and Statistics); the Spring 2026 sections were taught by Jagabathula and Lobel

The closest cousin to this course: a business school, no formal prerequisites, hands-on from day one. An MBA elective that also appears in the MS in Business Analytics and AI bulletin. Six three-hour sessions (building an agent; agent architecture; the power of agentic AI; enterprise agents and evaluation; deploying agents, with an industry panel; demo day). Students ship a working agent on an n8n backend with a front end, submit weekly one-page deliverables, and peer-review two demos. Public: the course site and the syllabus, with a student-projects page promised.

NYU Tandon · IE-GY 9113 · Fall 2026

System Integration: From ERP to Agentic AI

Emanuele Cimica (Technology Management)

The information-systems angle on agents: ERP is the baseline, and the course is about designing, integrating and using business systems with AI-driven capabilities layered on top. Fourteen sessions in four modules, project-based: student teams build an incremental AI integration solution and present it in a final competition judged by industry partners. Open to all NYU graduate students, Stern included; a public syllabus is linked from the program page.

MIT Media Lab · MAS.664 (also MAS.665, EC.731, IDS.865) · Spring 2026

AI Studio: AI Agents and Agentic Web

Ramesh Raskar

A studio course: teams of two or three build autonomous agents that plan, coordinate and execute complex workflows, covering agent design patterns, agent-to-agent communication, browser automation and decentralized coordination. Venture-style, with weekly mentor meetings, startup coaches and a Demo Day in May; the stated goal is an MVP at seed stage. Light on mechanism, heavy on shipping. Earlier iterations ran as AI Venture Studio and AI for Impact.

University of Virginia · CS 6501 · Spring 2026

Workshop on Building AI Agents

Henry Kautz

A hands-on graduate workshop in which much of the programming happens in class. It starts with small open-source models that run on a free Colab GPU or a laptop, then moves to hosted models and weight fine-tuning (QLoRA); one paper a week, a GitHub portfolio review, and a final project worth over half the grade. The closest in spirit to our local-first weeks. The same site publishes the interactive Transformer Logit Lens ↗ linked from our Week 9 page.

Carnegie Mellon · 24-880 · Spring 2026

AI Agents for Engineers

Amir Barati Farimani (Mechanical Engineering)

An applied course for graduate students and advanced undergraduates in engineering: LLM fundamentals, prompting, RAG and function calling, then agents for data analysis, simulation control, documentation and code synthesis, with examples from mechanical design, materials discovery and computational biology. Prior AI or machine-learning coursework and Python are expected.

Stanford · CS 146S · Fall 2026 / Fall 2025

The Modern Software Developer

Mihail Eric

Not an agents course in name, but a full public treatment of working with coding agents: MCP, agent skills, spec-driven development, "loop engineering" and the software factory, taught through weekly lectures, hands-on coding sessions and industry guests. The syllabus and the Fall 2025 archive are public. Pairs with our Week 2.

Stanford · CS 329T

Building and evaluating agentic AI applications

Anupam Datta, John Mitchell

A project-based course (the project is 70% of the grade) on building and evaluating agentic applications on foundation models: background in foundation models, prompting and RAG, then agent architectures, with evaluation methods for each. Slides and homeworks are posted; lectures are not recorded. The Autumn 2024 offering is archived on the site.

Where this course sits

What we do differently, on purpose

Business undergraduates

No systems sequence assumed. Group work has a code path (plain Python on a local model) and a no-code path (LangSmith Fleet), and the course asks the manager's questions first: who owns control flow, what does a run cost, when is it trusted unattended.

Local first

From Week 5 the agents run on a small model on your own laptop, so there is no API bill, no key to leak, and the unreliability of small models becomes the week's lesson instead of a surprise.

Security and governance carry weight

Evaluation (Week 8), security (Week 10) and agentic commerce (Week 11) are full weeks, and the capstone is graded on an eval harness and a security review, not only on whether the demo runs.
