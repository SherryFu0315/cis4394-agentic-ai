Where the skills go

Which jobs use what you learned this semester.

You can build an agent, measure whether it works, keep it from doing harm, and say whether it should exist at all. This page puts those four abilities next to the occupation data that exists as of November 2026: OpenAI’s map of 921 occupations, the 44 occupations whose real work products are now used to grade models, what people actually ask ChatGPT to do at work, the task-exposure scores from the GPTs-are-GPTs study, BLS projections to 2034, and job postings. Every number is dated and linked at the bottom.

Read it this way: in the consumer product, AI at work is mostly a writing and editing assistant. The agentic work this course teaches, programming, resolving computer problems and gathering information through tools, is under 8% of work messages. The agents that do those things for other people are built by a small group, and that is the group you are joining.

Work is a shrinking share of consumer use

Globally, 51.6% of consumer messages were work-related in July 2024. By September 2026 it was 32.2%, and 33.1% in the United States. Consumer use grew faster outside work. Enterprise traffic is not in this dataset, so this is not a measure of AI at work overall.

At work, people ask the model to produce

Among work-related messages in September 2026, 47.8% were classified as doing (produce an output), 27.3% as asking (give me information) and 24.8% as expressing. Outside work the order flips: 41.5% asking, 27.3% doing. Work use is delegation, which is the whole premise of an agent.

Georgia ranks seventh

Among U.S. states by consumer ChatGPT messages per capita, Georgia ranked 10th in the first quarter of 2025 and 7th in each of the last three quarters through September 2026. Technical help is the most work-heavy topic: 57% of technical-help messages are work-related, against 49% for writing.

Evidence 2 · OpenAI AI Jobs Transition Framework

Four paths for 921 occupations

Published April 25, 2026, the framework stops at a question most exposure studies skip. For each O*NET occupation it asks three things: can AI do a meaningful share of the tasks; does a person still have to deliver, supervise or answer for the work; and if AI makes the service cheaper, does demand grow enough to absorb the productivity gain. The answers sort occupations into four archetypes. OpenAI’s own words: these are not predictions that a percentage of jobs will disappear. They are a map of where change may show up first.

U.S. employment by archetype · 2024 · from the public occupation table (923 rows, 152.1 million jobs)
  • Jobs with less immediate change449 occupations · mostly physical, place-bound work
    71.9M47%
  • Jobs that will reorganize232 occupations · a person still has to sign off or be present
    35.3M23%
  • Jobs at higher automation risk91 occupations · standardized information work, no human-necessity flag
    26.9M18%
  • Jobs that grow with AI151 occupations · demand elasticity near or above 1
    18.0M12%

The number that does the sorting is the labor-demand elasticity in the last column of the table. Above 1 means a 10% fall in cost brings more than 10% more demand, so the occupation adds people as it gets more productive. The highest values in the whole table, 1.15, belong to computer systems analysts and computer network architects. Software developers are at 1.13, data scientists at 1.12. Customer service representatives are at 0.68, general office clerks at 0.41.

Where two official sources disagree, and why that is the lesson. The framework puts information security analysts in the higher-automation-risk group (exposure 0.54, no human-necessity flag, elasticity 0.68). BLS projects the same occupation to grow 28.5% by 2034, the second-fastest rate in its computer group. Both can be right: the framework measures how much of today’s task list a model can do; BLS measures how many people employers expect to hire. The security analyst whose tasks are automatable is the one who writes rules; the one BLS is counting is the one who decides what the agent may touch (Week 10). The same split shows up in the Week 11 purchasing agent: the task list of a customer service representative is 70% exposed, and BLS projects 153,700 fewer of them by 2034. The person who builds and supervises that agent is a different job.

Evidence 3 · GDPval

The 44 occupations whose real deliverables now grade the models

GDPval takes the nine sectors that contribute most to U.S. GDP and, within each, the five highest-earning occupations whose work is mostly digital. Professionals with an average of 14 years of experience wrote 1,320 tasks from their own jobs, each with reference files and a finished deliverable: a spreadsheet, a brief, a deck, a care plan. A second expert then compares a model’s deliverable with the human one, blind, and records win, tie or loss. The 220 public tasks are in the course’s local archive. What the score means for you

In the September 2025 paper the best model’s deliverable was rated as good as or better than the expert’s on 47.6% of tasks; the next model was at 38.8%, and a 2024 model at 12.4%. The leaderboard at evals.openai.com has moved since. Two things follow. The deliverable, not the chat, is the unit employers will judge. And a blind pairwise comparison by a domain expert is exactly the harness you built in Week 8 and the rubric you wrote for Group Assignment 3. In most of these 44 occupations the person who can set up and read that comparison does not yet exist on the org chart.

What it cannot tell you

GDPval grades one-shot deliverables from a clean prompt and reference files. It does not test whether a model can find the task, ask the right clarifying question, or recover when a tool fails, which is most of what an agent does and most of Weeks 4 to 10. A high score here says the writing and analysis are competitive. It says nothing about the loop around them.

Evidence 4 · job postings

How this shows up in hiring

The titles are old, the skills are new

Lightcast’s July 2026 read of U.S. postings: more than 86,000 jobs in 2025 asked for agentic AI skills, up more than 280% in a year. The median advertised salary was $153,000, against $141,000 for AI postings in general and $125,000 for IT. Fortune 1000 firms account for about 30% of agentic postings, more than double their 12% share of all postings; Amazon, Microsoft, KPMG and Accenture are in the top ten. Six of the ten occupations that ask most often are technical: AI engineers, software developers, machine learning engineers, NLP engineers. The authors’ own summary is that agentic AI is being built inside existing jobs, and if you looked only at job titles you would barely see it.

Where to look, and what to write

  • Titles to search: AI engineer, agent engineer, applied AI engineer, forward-deployed engineer, solutions engineer (AI), AI product manager, AI evaluation or quality engineer, AI governance or risk analyst, automation analyst, systems analyst.
  • Terms recruiters’ filters match: LangGraph, LangChain, LangSmith, Model Context Protocol, tool calling, function calling, RAG, evaluation harness, pass^k, LLM-as-judge, prompt injection, guardrails, human-in-the-loop, OWASP Agentic Top 10, NIST AI RMF.
  • The line that proves it: one sentence on your capstone with a number in it. “Built a LangGraph purchasing agent with four tools and an approval gate; pass^5 of 0.72 on 40 traced tasks; blocked 9 of 9 injected instructions.” That sentence is the whole semester, and it is checkable.
  • Lightcast’s other number: in the first quarter of 2026, AI occupations were 0.3% of all postings and only 7% of AI adoption. The other 93% was existing occupations adding AI skills. Most of the jobs on this page are in that 93%.
Read with care

What these datasets can and cannot say

Usage statistics

Signals

Aggregated, differentially private shares of consumer messages. They show which activities people hand to ChatGPT, not whether the output was used, and nothing about enterprise or API traffic, which is where agents run.

Exposure and classification

GPTs are GPTs · the framework

Both start from O*NET task lists. Exposure measures what a model could do in 2023; the framework adds human necessity and demand elasticity in 2026. Neither is an observation of jobs that changed. OpenAI’s blog text and its public table do not always agree on an occupation’s archetype; this page uses the table.

Task performance

GDPval

Expert-graded deliverables on well-specified tasks, released by the company whose models are graded, with a public leaderboard that also lists competitors. It measures output quality, not the finding, scoping, asking and recovering that fill a workday.
