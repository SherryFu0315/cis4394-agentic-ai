Week 10 in one sentence per page

Assume injection succeeds. Make sure it cannot do much, and make sure you would see it if it did. Every page this week was a way of taking that sentence seriously.

PageThe one thing to remember
01 · How agents get attackedPrompt injection is not breaking the model. It is writing to it: an email, a web page, a repo file, a tool result. The lethal trifecta names the three capabilities an attack needs (private data, untrusted content, a way to send things out); remove one leg and the worst case shrinks. The OWASP Agentic Top 10 gives you the vocabulary, the five stacked defenses give you the architecture, and the four 2025 cases show it already happening in products you use.
02 · Governance & frameworksThe NIST AI RMF is voluntary and tells you how to organize the work: Govern, Map, Measure, Manage. The EU AI Act is binding and tells you what you owe, by risk tier. Same agent, two lenses, and you need both to answer “are we allowed to ship this, and would we know if it misbehaved?” Almost none of it is new technology: least privilege, approval workflows, segregation of duties and audit trails are controls your MIS courses already taught.
03 · Practice & caseYou made a real agent follow a hidden instruction on your own laptop and watched the guard line fail. Then you attacked and patched a fictional inbox assistant on paper, where the strongest patch was a removed capability, not an added filter. Then you ran your own capstone through the six security-review questions. That checklist is the core of Milestone III.

Milestones II and III, in one place

Both briefs are in the iCollege dropboxes of your own section, with the exact dates. If anything here differs from iCollege, iCollege wins. The short version follows, because the two are designed as a pair: II makes your agent public and measured, III makes it complete and defended.

Milestone II · 5 pts · individual · due Week 12

A public link, an eval harness, a governance note

  • A public link to your agent (2 pts). Anyone with the URL can chat with it in a browser: no login, nothing to install. Behind the link is your Milestone I agent plus at least one expansion: a second real tool, memory or RAG, or a second agent with a written reason. The link must be open during our Week 12 class, when we will all try each other’s agents, and when you submit. Fallback: a 2–3 minute screen recording of one full run.
  • An eval harness with pass^k (2 pts). At least 10 cases with known-good answers, at least 2 of them edge or adversarial. Run every case at least 3 times. Report pass@1 and pass^k side by side and explain the failures you read. Include the harness or dataset so a grader can re-run it.
  • A governance note, half a page (1 pt). What data the agent reads and stores, and where. Where the human gate sits: which action, between which two steps, what the screen shows. What a stranger with your link can and cannot make the agent do. One named person accountable for it: you.
  • Plus an updated README and one full trace of the expanded agent.

Grading (5 pts): link opens for anyone 1 · expansion visible in it 1 · eval harness with pass^k 2 · governance note 1.

Milestone III · 5 pts · individual · due Week 13, with Implementation

The complete system and its security review

  • The complete system (2 pts). Runs from a clean clone by following your README. At least two tools. The eval harness from Milestone II updated to the final system.
  • The security review, one page for a manager (2 pts). Built on the six questions from Exercise B: every untrusted input that reaches the model, named; the lethal-trifecta analysis for your agent with one specific exfiltration path and the leg you removed or gated; 2–3 OWASP Agentic categories in your own words; at least 3 specific, layered guardrails, each tied to a leg; a deploy recommendation justified by your eval and your security findings.
  • Honest limitations (1 pt). What the agent cannot do, the failure modes you observed in the eval, and what you would change with another month.

Grading (5 pts): complete system meeting all hard requirements 2 · security review 2 · limitations 1. The Implementation grade (20 pts: works live 6 · design 5 · real eval 4 · robustness 3 · craft 2) is assessed on this same submission plus your Week 14 live demo.

How to make the public link · the Week 9 way

Five steps, one flag

  1. Run your app with sharing on.
    Mac / Linux: SHARE=1 python3 8_app_router.py (or your own app file) Windows PowerShell: $env:SHARE="1"; python 8_app_router.py

    If you wrote your own Gradio app: app.launch(share=True).

  2. Copy the line Running on public URL: https://xxxx.gradio.live. That is your link.
  3. Test it on your phone with Wi-Fi off. If it loads on cellular data, it is public.
  4. Keep the laptop on, plugged in and awake for as long as the link must work. Mac: run caffeinate -i in a second terminal. Windows: Settings → System → Power → Screen and sleep → Never when plugged in.
  5. A link lasts 7 days. Restarting the app gives a new link, so update your submission if it changes.

If you see “Could not create share link”, campus Wi-Fi may be blocking it: connect the laptop to your phone’s hotspot and try again. No-code path (Fleet or another platform): use the platform’s Publish or Share feature; the link must open without a login. If your platform cannot do that, talk to me before the deadline. The same-Wi-Fi and public-link methods are written out step by step on Week 9, page 03.

Before you share: a public link means anyone can click your Approve button.
  • Connect nothing real: no real email, payments, deletions or company data. Stub tools or a sandbox only.
  • Keys live in environment variables, never in the UI, the repo or the recording.
  • Put caps on cost: max tokens per answer, max tool calls per turn, max turns per session.
  • Keep the human gate visible in the chat, showing the arguments, not a summary.
  • Expect classmates to try prompt injection on your agent. That is allowed on the links your classmates share for this course and nowhere else. Save what worked: it is the raw material for your Milestone III security review.
